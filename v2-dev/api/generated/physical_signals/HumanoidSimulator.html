Description#

A physically based humanoid that follows a captured skeleton.

The captured joints are replaced by the pose of a virtual body: a humanoid of 21 masses (the joints of HUMANOID_JOINTS) joined by 20 springs (the bones of HUMANOID_BONES) is integrated in time. Every bone is a spring with a length force, which keeps its rest length, and a rotation force, which turns it towards the direction observed in the input; the head is the (very heavy) root. The simulated pose is smooth and always articulated, with the proportions of the model body, so the feature acts as a virtual sensor that removes the jitter and the dropouts of a captured skeleton and yields a physically plausible joint trajectory. It is not a noise filter in the signal-processing sense (see Notes).

process(skeleton, control=None, *, timestamp=None) takes the captured joints, a dict label -> (x, y, z) or a (21, 3) array in the order of labels. The optional control holds bone directions, a (20, 3) array in the order of HUMANOID_BONES or a dict "<joint1>-<joint2>" -> (x, y, z); the bones then follow them instead of the directions seen in the joints. They are directions relative to the parent bone, the convention of the output of HumanoidOrientations: passing that output as control reproduces the pose of the joints it came from.

Parameters:
labelssequence of str, optional

Names of the rows of array frames (live). Default None: the rows follow the order of HUMANOID_JOINTS.

joint_mapdict, optional

joint of the model -> label of your data (live); the model names are those of HUMANOID_JOINTS and the names you leave out keep their own. The constructor raises ValueError when labels lack a joint the model needs.

start_from_inputbool, default False

Put the model on the first frame after construction or reset() instead of letting it settle there from its T-pose (live). The present joints take the input positions, the rest lengths of the bones become the bone lengths of the input, and that first frame is returned as it is. A change applies from the next reset().

reference_directiontuple of float, default (1.0, 0.0, 0.0)

Reference direction of the model (live): the direction from which the rest directions of the springs are measured (dimensionless; the x axis by default).

integration_stepfloat, default 0.001

Step of the numerical integration in seconds, > 0 (live). A call that advances the model by dt seconds runs int(dt / integration_step) steps.

gravitytuple of float, default (0.0, 0.0, 0.0)

Gravity vector in units per second squared (live); zero means no gravity.

dampingfloat, default 10.0

Damping coefficient of the springs (live). The EyesWeb default, 0.3, makes chains of four or five bones diverge; 10 is stable with noisy input and 3 follows clean data with less lag.

viscosityfloat, default 0.0

Viscous friction of the masses (live).

length_forcesbool, default True

Switch on the forces that keep each spring at its rest length (live).

rotation_forcesbool, default True

Switch on the forces that turn each spring towards its target direction (live).

ground_interactionbool, default False

Add a floor, the plane through floor_position whose normal is opposite to gravity (live); a mass below it is pushed back along the normal.

floor_positiontuple of float, default (0.0, 0.0, 0.0)

A point of the floor plane, in units (live).

sampling_ratefloat, optional

Frame rate in Hz, used for the time step of a call that has no timestamp (live). Default None (no hidden rate): the step is then 1 s and reason says "no time given: per-sample derivatives".

units{“m”, “mm”}, default “m”

Unit of your frames, of the outputs, of gravity and of floor_position (live). See Notes.

Notes

Outputs. joints: (21, 3) array of (x, y, z) in units, labelled by HUMANOID_JOINTS; a joint the model did not produce is NaN. The result is OK on every valid call (there is no warm-up). A frame with an infinite value, an empty frame, a frame without any joint (every row NaN) and a dict frame whose keys match none of the names the model reads are INVALID_INPUT (the cause is in result.reason); a joint that is missing (absent from the dict, or a row with a NaN) is a dropout the model handles.

Time. The time step of a call is timestamp - previous timestamp in seconds, else 1 / sampling_rate, else 1 s. A timestamp that does not increase gives INVALID_INPUT ("non-increasing timestamp") and leaves the model where it was; a non-finite timestamp raises ValueError. The first timed call after construction or reset() uses 1 / sampling_rate (or 1 s), never the absolute time.

Units. This is the one feature that rescales data. The EyesWeb body is defined in millimetres and the spring forces depend on the length unit, so the feature works internally in millimetres and converts at the boundary: with units="m" frames are multiplied by 1000 on the way in and joints divided by 1000 on the way out, with "mm" nothing is converted. A body driven in metres reproduces the millimetre run up to rounding; metres declared as "mm" give a different motion, because the model is not scale free.

Behaviour. The head is pinned: the output is the pose of a body hanging from where its head starts, the T-pose at the origin or, with start_from_input, the first frame; the translation of the person is not followed, as in EyesWeb. Bone lengths stay those of the body and only the directions follow the input. A joint missing in a frame leaves its bones unchanged. Every parameter is live; reset() puts the body back in the T-pose.

Known limitation. The rotation springs make chains of four or five bones flutter at a low damping, and the model is not a noise filter: it follows the input with a lag that grows with damping.

Differences from EyesWeb. The default damping is 10 (EyesWeb: 0.3, which diverges); time is in seconds per call; control is read as directions relative to the parent bone (EyesWeb read world directions, so there the output of its orientation extractor gave another pose).

Corrected in the core. The damping force is proportional to the relative velocity of the two masses (the original used speed magnitudes and injected energy); the feet of the T-pose are built along the direction they rest in, so the body at rest is in equilibrium; the reset to the input, which the original could never reach, works and moves only the joints that are present.

Examples

>>> import numpy as np
>>> from pyeyesweb.physical_signals import HumanoidSimulator
>>> simulator = HumanoidSimulator(sampling_rate=25.0)
>>> result = simulator.process({"head": (0.0, 1.7, 0.0)})    # a lone joint: the body hangs from it, in its T-pose
>>> result.joints.shape
(21, 3)
>>> follower = HumanoidSimulator(sampling_rate=25.0, start_from_input=True)
>>> bool(np.allclose(follower.process(result.joints).joints, result.joints))
True
