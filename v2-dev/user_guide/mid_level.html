Mid-level features#

Layer 3 describes a gesture: a movement unit that lasts from half a second to about three seconds, with a beginning and an end. Where a low-level feature gives a value for each frame, a mid-level feature needs the unit as a whole, or the time over which a quantity builds up, and gives one value for the gesture: where it starts and ends, how long it lasts, how sudden or impulsive it is, how much of the space it occupied. The layer has 7 features; this page helps to choose among them. The background is in Layers and the catalog and Conceptual framework.

Which feature for which question#

Where are the gestures? GestureSegmenter turns an intensity signal, typically the kinetic energy of a skeleton, into the start and the end of gestures, with an optional flag that keeps noise from opening a gesture. MotionDuration measures the length of the motion phases and of the pauses of its output. Most of the other features of this layer are computed over a gesture, so segmentation is usually the first step. Theory: Gesture segmentation.

How sudden or impulsive is it? Suddenness fits a stable distribution to the speeds of a trajectory and tells how heavy the tail of the distribution is, that is how often the movement has abrupt speed changes. SuddennessImpulsivity gives the direction change of the trajectory when it is also sudden, and ContractionImpulsivity the change per second of the contraction index over a short, energetic gesture. Theory: Impulsivity and suddenness. The quality of impulsivity is defined in [3].

How is the space occupied? PositionDependingPotentials and RegionDependingPotentials turn the grid cells hit by a point (HitCellDetector, layer 1) into potentials with a memory of past hits: a cell that is visited often keeps a high potential and the potential decays where nobody goes. Theory: Shape, symmetry and balance.

A typical pipeline#

The mid-level features are fed by the outputs of layers 1 and 2. A point makes two reaches of 0.4 m, with pauses before and between them. Its kinetic energy is the intensity that the segmenter takes, and the segmentation signal goes to MotionDuration.

>>> import numpy as np
>>> from pyeyesweb.low_level import KineticEnergy
>>> from pyeyesweb.mid_level import GestureSegmenter, MotionDuration
>>> rate = 50.0
>>> time = np.arange(0.0, 8.0, 1.0 / rate)
>>> def reach(start, stop, amplitude):
...     s = np.clip((time - start) / (stop - start), 0.0, 1.0)
...     return amplitude * (10 * s**3 - 15 * s**4 + 6 * s**5)      # minimum-jerk profile
>>> x = reach(1.0, 3.0, 0.4) - reach(5.0, 7.0, 0.4)
>>> position = np.column_stack([x, np.zeros_like(x), np.zeros_like(x)])
>>> energy = KineticEnergy(labels=["hand"], weights={"hand": 1.0}, sampling_rate=rate).run(position[:, None, :])
>>> round(float(energy.kinetic_energy.max()), 3)
0.07
>>> segmentation = GestureSegmenter(firing_threshold=0.005).run(energy.kinetic_energy)
>>> (np.flatnonzero(segmentation.is_gesture_start) / rate).tolist()     # seconds
[1.42, 5.42]
>>> (np.flatnonzero(segmentation.is_gesture_end) / rate).tolist()
[2.82, 6.82]
>>> durations = MotionDuration(sampling_rate=rate).run(segmentation.is_gesturing.astype(float))
>>> float(np.nanmax(durations.motion_duration)), float(np.nanmax(durations.pause_duration))
(1.4, 2.6)

The first gesture is found from 1.42 s to 2.82 s, although the reach lasts from 1.0 s to 3.0 s: the segmenter smooths the intensity and declares a gesture while it is at least firing_threshold (in the unit of the intensity), so the start is late by a few samples and the slow beginning and end of a reach, where the energy is low, are left out. The threshold fixes how much of the movement counts as a gesture. The gestures can then be cut from the signals of layer 2 and summarised, for example with SpectralArcLength on the speed of each gesture, or measured by the suddenness features.

What to know before choosing#

  • A mid-level feature takes the signals of one unit of movement, but it does not cut them for you: most of them work on a window you size to the gesture, in samples, or on a signal that you segmented.

  • The time-dependent features take sampling_rate or timestamps; without a rate the durations are in samples (Time, units and parameters).

  • The potentials need the cells of HitCellDetector, a layer-1 feature, and keep a memory across calls: reset() them between recordings.

Hands-on: see Gestures and qualities.
