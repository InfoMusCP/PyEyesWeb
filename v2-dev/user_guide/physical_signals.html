Physical signals#

Layer 1 of the conceptual model (Conceptual framework) holds the signals that come from sensors and the virtual sensors built on them. In PyEyesWeb a feature of this layer takes raw frames or a model and gives a signal again, so that the features of the upper layers can work on cleaner or richer data: it extracts centroids or parts from an image, or locates points in a grid. Its timescale is the frame: a value belongs to one call, not to a window. This page helps to choose among the 4 features of the layer; the layers are described in Layers and the catalog.

Which feature for which problem#

The features fall in two groups, one for each kind of input.

You have video and want the body without a skeleton. The silhouette of a person is a binary mask.

  • SilhouetteCentroids for 14 points of a body (centre of gravity, head, shoulders, elbows, hands, knees, feet), cheap enough to follow at the frame rate of a camera.

  • SilhouetteBodyParts for the bounding box and the mask of six parts (head, trunk, arms, legs), when you want to analyse a part of the image, not a point.

  • Videogram to summarise a long video in one image in which a movement is a visible trace, for inspection or for a visual feature of the movement over time.

Theory: Imaging. The outline of a silhouette is measured by the contour features of layer 2.

You have positions in a plane and want to know where they fall. HitCellDetector divides a rectangle into a grid and gives the cell of each point. Its theory is in Shape, symmetry and balance.

A typical pipeline#

The pipeline of the layer goes from an image to the measures of an outline: a mask, then SilhouetteCentroids or the contour of the mask, then the contour features of Low-level features.

What to know before choosing#

  • The features of this layer do not wait for data: every valid call is OK, with no warm-up (see Results, statuses and recorders).

  • The image features and HitCellDetector have no time axis: a value belongs to one call.

  • Image features take 8-bit images or masks and return pixel coordinates (see Inputs).

Hands-on: see Physical signals: points in a grid (points) and Physical signals: images (images).
