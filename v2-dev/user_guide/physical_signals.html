Physical signals#

Layer 1 of the conceptual model (Conceptual framework) holds the signals that come from sensors and the virtual sensors built on them. In PyEyesWeb a feature of this layer takes raw frames or a model and gives a signal again, so that the features of the upper layers can work on cleaner or richer data: it simulates a body, extracts centroids or parts from an image, or locates points in a grid. Its timescale is the frame: a value belongs to one call, not to a window. This page helps to choose among the 11 features of the layer; the layers are described in Layers and the catalog.

Which feature for which problem#

The features fall in three groups, one for each kind of input.

You have a skeleton and want a cleaner or a forecast one. The captured joints jitter and drop out; a mass-spring model follows them and gives a smooth, articulated pose with constant bone lengths.

  • HumanoidSimulator for the fixed 21-joint humanoid (the joints of the first Kinect SDK as EyesWeb names them). It is the choice for a body captured by any system whose joints you can map to those names (see Map your marker set to a skeleton).

  • HumanoidOrientations when you need the posture itself, the direction of each bone in the frame of its parent, which does not depend on where the body is.

  • GenericModelSimulator, GenericModelOrientations, GenericModelPredictor and GenericModelEstimator when the structure is not a human body (a limb, a puppet, an instrument) or you want to predict a movement or identify the parameters of a body. They take a MassSpringModel.

  • PointsPredictor for the simplest forecast of any labelled points, a constant-velocity extrapolation one frame ahead, with no model.

Theory, assumptions and the units exception: Humanoid and mass-spring models.

You have video and want the body without a skeleton. The silhouette of a person is a binary mask.

  • SilhouetteCentroids for 14 points of a body (centre of gravity, head, shoulders, elbows, hands, knees, feet), cheap enough to follow at the frame rate of a camera.

  • SilhouetteBodyParts for the bounding box and the mask of six parts (head, trunk, arms, legs), when you want to analyse a part of the image, not a point.

  • Videogram to summarise a long video in one image in which a movement is a visible trace, for inspection or for a visual feature of the movement over time.

Theory: Imaging. The outline of a silhouette is measured by the contour features of layer 2.

You have positions in a plane and want to know where they fall. HitCellDetector divides a rectangle into a grid and gives the cell of each point. It is the first step of the space-occupation features of layer 3 (Mid-level features); its theory is in Shape, symmetry and balance.

A typical pipeline#

A skeleton passes through the simulator and then to the features of layer 2. The simulator is driven by three captured frames, with a joint missing in the second one (a dropout); it gives the 21 joints at every call, and the simulated joints, labelled like the model, feed any feature that takes a labelled skeleton.

>>> import numpy as np
>>> from pyeyesweb.physical_signals import HUMANOID_JOINTS, HumanoidSimulator
>>> from pyeyesweb.low_level import KineticEnergy
>>> simulator = HumanoidSimulator(sampling_rate=25.0)
>>> energy = KineticEnergy(labels=list(HUMANOID_JOINTS), weights={name: 1.0 for name in HUMANOID_JOINTS}, sampling_rate=25.0)
>>> tpose = simulator.process({"head": (0.0, 0.0, 0.0)}).joints
>>> frame = {name: tuple(point) for name, point in zip(HUMANOID_JOINTS, tpose)}
>>> second = {name: point for name, point in frame.items() if name != "left_hand"}    # dropout
>>> statuses = []
>>> for captured in (frame, second, frame):
...     simulated = simulator.process(captured)
...     statuses.append((simulated.status.name, bool(np.isfinite(simulated.joints).all())))
>>> statuses
[('OK', True), ('OK', True), ('OK', True)]
>>> energy.process(dict(zip(HUMANOID_JOINTS, simulated.joints))).status.name
'OK'

The other pipeline of the layer goes from an image to the measures of an outline: a mask, then SilhouetteCentroids or the contour of the mask, then the contour features of Low-level features.

What to know before choosing#

  • The features of this layer do not wait for data: every valid call is OK, with no warm-up (see Results, statuses and recorders).

  • Time is in seconds. The simulators, the predictor and the estimator advance their model by the time of the call (Time, units and parameters); the image features and HitCellDetector have no time axis.

  • Units: HumanoidSimulator is the one feature of the library that converts data (its model is in millimetres and it converts at the boundary); the generic models do not. Humanoid and mass-spring models explains the difference.

  • Image features take 8-bit images or masks and return pixel coordinates (see Inputs).
