Low-level features#

Layer 2 describes a movement fragment: a single frame or a short window of up to about half a second. The values are time series at the rate of the input, and they are the quantities most analyses start from: how fast a point moves, how much energy a body spends, how contracted, symmetric or balanced a posture is, how smooth a speed profile is, what shape an outline has. The layer has 22 features; this page helps to choose among them and shows how they chain. The background is in Layers and the catalog and Conceptual framework.

Which feature for which question#

How does a point move? Kinematics gives the velocity, acceleration, jerk, curvature or direction of a point from a Savitzky-Golay fit, and is the usual first step: its speed feeds the smoothness metrics. For the energy of a whole body use KineticEnergy, the weighted kinetic energy of the joints of a skeleton. DirectionChange measures the sharp turns of a path in a window, Directness how straight a path is, and TrajectoryNonPlanarity how far a 3D path leaves its best-fit planes. MotionNoiseDetector tells whether the motion of a skeleton is random. Theory: Kinematics and energy.

How smooth is it? Start from a speed profile and pick the metric that suits the question. SpectralArcLength and LogDimensionlessJerk are the two established metrics [2]; SubmovementCount counts the speed peaks, Harmonicity compares a profile with a single bell-shaped peak, and CurvatureDispersion and CurvatureVelocityCorrelation work on the vectors of the movement. Theory: Smoothness.

How is the posture shaped? BoundingBoxFilledArea, EllipsoidSphericity and PointsDensity measure contraction and expansion; Symmetry and TriangleSymmetry the left/right symmetry; Equilibrium the balance of the barycentre between the feet. Theory: Shape, symmetry and balance.

What shape is an outline? FilterContour, ContourCurvature, ConvexHull and BodyPartsFromCurvature work on the closed contour of a silhouette, one frame at a time. Theory: Imaging.

Frame features and window features#

A frame feature gives a value for each call (for example KineticEnergy, Symmetry, the contour features). A window feature buffers the newest window samples itself and gives its first value when the window is full, with the status WARMING_UP before (the smoothness metrics, DirectionChange, CurvatureVelocityCorrelation). window is a number of samples and hop the stride between two values (see Results, statuses and recorders). Whatever the length of the window, the quantity measured is that of a movement fragment; for longer spans use layer 3.

A typical pipeline#

A point makes a smooth reach, its speed is measured, and a smoothness metric summarises the speed. The reach is the minimum-jerk profile \(x = 0.4\,(10 s^3 - 15 s^4 + 6 s^5)\), with \(s\) the fraction of the movement time elapsed.

>>> import numpy as np
>>> from pyeyesweb.low_level import Kinematics, SpectralArcLength
>>> rate = 50.0
>>> time = np.arange(0.0, 4.0, 1.0 / rate)
>>> s = np.clip((time - 1.0) / 2.0, 0.0, 1.0)
>>> x = 0.4 * (10 * s**3 - 15 * s**4 + 6 * s**5)                  # a 0.4 m reach between 1 s and 3 s
>>> position = np.column_stack([x, np.zeros_like(x), np.zeros_like(x)])
>>> speed = Kinematics(dims=3, sampling_rate=rate).run(position).velocity
>>> round(float(speed.max()), 2)
0.37
>>> arc = SpectralArcLength(window=100, sampling_rate=rate).run(speed)
>>> arc
Recorder(SpectralArcLength, rows=200, columns=['spectral_arc_length'], status={'OK': 101, 'WARMING_UP': 99})
>>> round(float(arc.spectral_arc_length[-1]), 2)
-1.63

Each step is a feature and the output of one is the input of the next. For a complete skeleton, KineticEnergy replaces Kinematics and gives a single intensity for the whole body, which is what the segmentation of Mid-level features takes. Compute the speed once and give the same array to as many metrics as you need.

What to know before choosing#

  • Give sampling_rate (or timestamps) to every feature that has a time in it; otherwise the values are per sample and the reason of the result says so (Time, units and parameters).

  • A feature with skeleton= or labels= and joint_map= reads a labelled skeleton; the presets and the mapping are in Map your marker set to a skeleton.

  • To run a whole recording through a feature, use run; it gives the same numbers as a loop of process.
